# Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B MLX error analysis (run `20260923T082226631235Z`)

## Scope and headline

Read-only analysis of the archived 100-case run (`cases.jsonl`, `judgments.jsonl`, `summary.json`); this is not a re-review of the judgments. The fixture's intended references and archived human assessments are treated as the evaluation record. No key was accessed. The run metadata identifies MLX on Apple Silicon, Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, and `jev-latest` (`metadata.json`).

The measured gap is broad, not just a single weak dimension. Stored summary/assessment labels report MLX per-question exact correctness of **45/100 owner, 53/100 urgent, 49/99 definitive impact** versus JEV's **82/100, 95/100, 81/100**. A separate raw-output/fixture comparison (see `corrected-metrics.json`) yields objective exact matches of **45/100 owner, 46/100 urgent, 50/100 impact** for MLX and **82/100, 95/100, 81/100** for JEV. MLX impact's 50th raw exact match is case-074: output class and reference are both low, while its archived assessment is indeterminate, so that judgment is excluded from the 49/99 exact-correct tally. MLX urgency's 53 stored correct labels overstate exact matches by seven (details below). The summary's case-level outcome (JEV 68 wins, MLX 7, tie 25) and overall labels (JEV 65 correct/1 incorrect/34 partial, MLX 14/19/67) are quoted as stored, not independently validated; the seven urgency discrepancies may affect derived aggregates. This says JEV more often matched the fixture references; it does not establish that every fixture label is objectively unambiguous.

## Failure patterns by decision

### Owner

MLX selected **other 21 times**, versus only 5 reference `other`s; only 2 of those 21 selections matched. It produced `operations` 34 times (16 correct, 18 wrong). By contrast it selected `safety_compliance` only 10 times (6 correct), although the reference had 22 such cases. It selected `technical_support` just 7 times (5 correct) for 20 references. Thus the characteristic misses are broad-function confusion and under-recognition of explicit security/safety/compliance and technical-system ownership—not random errors.

Largest reference→MLX error cells: `safety_compliance → operations` **8**, `technical_support → operations` **6**, `account_access → other` **5**, `safety_compliance → other` **5**, `technical_support → billing` **4**, and `sales → other` **4**. Examples include case-001 (medication-dose conflict, predicted operations rather than safety_compliance), case-007 (reported cross-patient discharge summary, predicted other), case-025 (missing lithium handling mark, predicted operations), case-028 (tablet printer fault, operations), case-071 (regional login outage, account_access rather than technical_support), and case-040 (pre-booking service-tier sales question, operations). These are examples of archived outputs, not newly adjudicated labels.

Owner confidence is weakly discriminative: mean reported confidence was **0.206** (0.249 on correct versus 0.171 on incorrect answers). The top-vs-runner-up probability margin averaged **0.25** on correct choices and **0.139** on wrong ones; overall median margin was **0.141**, p90 **0.423**, max **0.658**. Many errors are close, but not all: case-015 had confidence 0.394 while misrouting the unknown card charge to billing instead of the reference safety_compliance. A rule that merely abstains on low confidence could help some borderline cases, not fix confident semantic confusions.

### Urgency

This is the clearest systematic failure: MLX's archived scalar urgency values were **0.616–0.926 (mean 0.860)**, and all 100 normalized outputs in both archive and judgment ledger were `true`. The reference split is 46 true / 54 false, so direct output/reference agreement is **46/100**, with **54 false positives, zero false negatives**. The stored judgments and summary instead label 53 as correct / 47 incorrect, overstating correctness by seven. In exactly seven rows—**case-056, case-057, case-073, case-074, case-078, case-080, case-088**—reference is false and prediction is true, but `questions.urgent.assessment.mlx.correctness` says `correct`; each rationale itself describes why the case is not urgent. Thus these are assessment-label inconsistencies, not seven normalized predictions that disagree with the raw archive. Raw scalar, normalized prediction, and judgment-ledger prediction agree on all 100 cases. Case-008 (one missed upload, device resumed syncing, no symptoms) yielded just 0.04 confidence but still `true`; case-052 (stock covers a delayed part) yielded 0.14 confidence and `true`; routine cases such as case-020, case-069 and case-100 also went urgent. Conversely the score gap is not attributable to the response threshold alone: all model values are well above 0.5, and most are far above it.

### Impact

The distribution collapses toward the extremes: MLX's most likely class was **high in 76/100 and low in 24/100; never moderate**. Of 50 definitive wrong answers, **25 were low→high**, **17 moderate→high**, 5 moderate→low, and 3 high→low; one further judgment is indeterminate. This strongly overstates severity, especially for mundane/routine cases and contained disruptions. Examples: case-010 (copy of paid copay receipt) predicted high; case-030 (preorder billing timing) high; case-057 (recoverable packaging-cell stops, output on schedule) high; case-059 (request for an already-shipped coating's safety data sheet) high. Understatement exists too: case-011 (duplicate wire before cutoff) predicted low, and case-068 (downed wire) predicted low.

Reference class counts are low 41, moderate 22, high 37. Mean MLX probability vectors by reference class (low/moderate/high) were: low-reference **0.402/0.109/0.489**, moderate-reference **0.388/0.126/0.486**, high-reference **0.311/0.095/0.594**. The high score's Brier score was **0.341** versus JEV **0.152**. These vectors show some separation for true high impact, but also substantial high mass on low/moderate references. In the schema, the score is the probability-weighted numeric value (low=0, moderate=1, high=2), then normalized to a class for evaluation; this expected-value/label boundary can compound a class-selection problem.

## Prompt, model, threshold, calibration, implementation

* **Measured prompt/template path:** compiler v1 builds query text as `Decision: {prompt}\nState: {canonical JSON state}`, then applies manifest query template `Instruct: {instruction}\nQuery:{text}`. Choice candidates are `{label}: {criterion}`; score candidates likewise use label and criterion. Binary urgency uses only two generic candidate texts: `unsupported false no absent not required can wait` and `supported true yes present urgent today immediate required`. Candidate template is `{text}` (manifest and `crates/decengine-compiler/src/lib.rs`). This is a sensible embedding-model instruction/query pattern, but the binary urgency candidates are bags of loosely related synonyms, not a calibrated answer rubric. They encode words like “urgent/today/immediate” but little of the actual prompt's harm/interruption/deadline test. That is a strong measured design concern, not proof of causal attribution.
* **Thresholding:** MLX computes softmax cosine similarities at temperature 0.05 (`crates/decengine-engine-mlx/src/lib.rs`), and the runtime returns the supported probability as the urgency scalar (`crates/decengine-runtime/src/lib.rs`). Archive normalized booleans are all true; the minimum scalar 0.616 is above 0.5. Raising a threshold above the observed range would flip every output false on this run, recovering 54 false references but losing all 46 true ones; it would not provide a useful split. This is a ranking/representation problem as well as a possible calibration problem.
* **Calibration/confidence:** Brier scores (where defined) are worse for MLX owner **0.277 vs 0.111 JEV** and impact **0.341 vs 0.152**. MLX reported confidence is normalized inverse entropy over candidate probabilities, not an empirically calibrated probability of correctness (implementation at `normalized_entropy_confidence`). Low average confidence—owner 0.206, impact 0.176, urgency 0.426—does not itself change the argmax/score output. Thus “confidence low” is not an implemented safety threshold. Urgency scalar output is also distinct from confidence; its all-positive values are direct evidence of class collapse.
* **Forward/config/tokenization observed in source:** manifest specifies Qwen3 0.6B, last-token pooling, L2 normalization, left-padding, 32,768 max length, and above query template. MLX tokenizes with `tokenizer.encode(text, true)`, rejects overlength inputs (does not truncate), runs the decoder, selects hidden state at `-1`, then L2-normalizes. The source implements Qwen3 causal attention with RoPE, q/k RMS norms, gated SiLU MLP, and final RMS norm. Candidate and query are each embedded independently; cosine dot products produce the softmax. Those choices are internally consistent with the declared embedding approach. Archived run statuses were all OK; no inference errors, empty/truncated contexts, or run-time tokenizer failures are indicated. No evidence in these artifacts isolates a forward-pass or tokenizer defect. Conversely, source inspection and successful execution are not an independent numerical parity proof against official Qwen outputs; they cannot rule out a subtle implementation/weight-conversion mismatch. The run's behavioral pattern alone is insufficient to claim such a bug.

## Reference ambiguity and limits

The case fixture calls its authored labels “intended_reference_for_evaluation_not_professional_advice,” and says cases are synthetic and based on common operational patterns. Ownership is inherently policy-dependent in cross-functional incidents (technical repair vs safety/compliance ownership, for example), so apparent owner “errors” can reflect reference conventions as well as model judgment. Impact severities and same-day urgency are also rubric-dependent. No judgments were re-reviewed here, per instruction; the urgency check above compares stored labels, references, normalized predictions, and raw scalars only. In addition to the seven MLX urgency-label inconsistencies, the impact assessment for case-074 is `indeterminate` despite normalized prediction `low` matching reference `low`; this is counted as indeterminate (not correct) in the stored summary and does not change the 49 exact-correct count. No normalized prediction/raw-output mismatches were found for MLX owner, urgency, or impact; no reference-vs-assessment correctness mismatches were found for JEV on those fields. The high rate of one-direction urgency predictions and high/low impact polarization are measured; causal explanations above are hypotheses based on the prompt/candidate design and distributions, not proof of label error or root cause. This is one 100-case run, not repeated-run evidence.

## Reproducibility

Stored summary totals and relative outcomes are in `summary.json`; because of the seven identified urgency assessment inconsistencies, its derived MLX aggregate/case-outcome values should not be treated as reconciled metrics. To reproduce predictions against references and expose assessment disagreements from this archive with Python (run from repository root):

```python
import json, collections
p = 'results/comparison-runs/20260923T082226631235Z/'
cases = {r['case_id']: r for r in map(json.loads, open(p+'cases.jsonl'))}
judgments = list(map(json.loads, open(p+'judgments.jsonl')))
for q in ('owner', 'urgent', 'impact'):
    print(q, 'reference → MLX', collections.Counter(
        (j['reference']['decision'][q], j['outputs']['mlx']['normalized_predictions'][q])
        for j in judgments))
    print(q, 'correctness-label disagreements', [
        j['case_id'] for j in judgments
        if (j['outputs']['mlx']['normalized_predictions'][q] == j['reference']['decision'][q])
        != (j['questions'][q]['assessment']['mlx']['correctness'] == 'correct')])
print('urgency scalar range:', min(cases[j['case_id']]['decengine']['raw_response']['results']['urgent']['value'] for j in judgments),
      max(cases[j['case_id']]['decengine']['raw_response']['results']['urgent']['value'] for j in judgments))
```

Relevant source references: `models/manifests/qwen3-embedding-0.6b.json`; `crates/decengine-compiler/src/lib.rs` (query/candidate construction); `crates/decengine-engine-mlx/src/lib.rs` (forward, embedding, cosine-softmax and entropy confidence); `crates/decengine-runtime/src/lib.rs` (choice argmax, Noul scalar, score expectation).
